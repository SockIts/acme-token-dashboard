User Guide

ACME Atomic Swaps

Trade ACME assets for Bitcoin in a single, trustless transaction. No counterparty risk, no escrow service, no waiting — just a cryptographically secure swap that either completes fully or not at all.

Concepts

How it Works

Atomic Swaps replace the old multi-step BTCPay settlement with a single Bitcoin transaction. The seller locks the asset on-chain; any buyer can complete the trade without the seller being online.

step 1 — seller
AtomicSell

Seller broadcasts a listing transaction that escrows the asset and creates a tiny 546-sat fill UTXO as an anchor.

step 2 — buyer
AtomicFill

Buyer adds BTC to the seller's pre-signed PSBT and broadcasts — one transaction settles the trade instantly.

anchor
Fill UTXO

A 546-sat dust output that binds the asset. Spending it by an AtomicFill credits the buyer; spending it any other way cancels the order.

protection
Seller Auth

A 64-byte Schnorr signature by the seller over the exact trade terms — preventing buyers from altering price, asset, or quantity.

⚡
Why this is better

Old BTCPay orders required 3 transactions and seller interaction at fill time. Atomic Swaps need just 2 transactions and the seller can be completely offline when the buyer fills.

Part 01: Creating a Listing →
Part 01 — Sellers

Creating a Listing

Listing an asset for sale takes two steps: broadcast an AtomicSell transaction to escrow the asset on-chain, then post your PSBT and signature to the marketplace so buyers can find and fill your order.

Step-by-Step

1
Choose your asset, quantity, and price

Navigate to Marketplace → Create Listing. Select the asset you want to sell, enter the quantity, and set your price in satoshis. Minimum price is 1,000 satoshis.

2
Set an expiration

Choose how long the listing stays open — from 1 block (~10 min) to 8,064 blocks (~8 weeks). If unfilled by expiry, the escrowed asset automatically returns to your wallet.

3
Sign the order transaction

Your wallet builds and signs the AtomicSell TX. This debits the asset from your balance and creates the on-chain fill UTXO. Broadcast it.

4
Sign the fill PSBT and seller_auth

After the order TX confirms, sign two more things: the fill template PSBT (with SIGHASH_SINGLE|ANYONECANPAY) and the seller_auth Schnorr signature over your trade terms. These prove you set the price and allow buyers to complete the trade.

5
Submit to the marketplace

Post your partial PSBT and seller_auth to the marketplace relay. Your listing is now live — buyers can fill it any time, with no further action needed from you.

⚠️
Asset is escrowed immediately

The moment your AtomicSell TX confirms, the asset is debited from your balance and held in escrow. You cannot send it elsewhere while the order is open — cancel the order first to get it back.

Listing Parameters

ParameterDescriptionConstraints
give_assetThe asset you are sellingMust exist · cannot be BTC
give_quantityQuantity to sellMust be positive · you must hold this balance

Part 02: Managing Orders →
Part 02 — Sellers

Managing Orders

Once a listing is live, you can track its status, cancel it at any time, or let it expire. Your asset is always safe — it returns to you if not filled.

Order Statuses

open
Active listing

Asset is escrowed and the listing is visible to buyers on the marketplace. Anyone can fill it.

filled
Trade complete

A buyer filled the order. BTC is in your wallet, asset has been transferred to the buyer.

cancelled
Cancelled by seller

You spent the fill UTXO in a non-fill transaction. The escrowed asset has been refunded to you.

expired
Expired unfilled

The order passed its expiry block. The escrowed asset has been automatically refunded to you.

How to Cancel an Order

To cancel, go to Marketplace → My Orders and click Cancel on an open listing. This broadcasts a transaction that spends the fill UTXO without being an AtomicFill — which the indexer detects as an implicit cancel and refunds your asset.

ℹ️
Race condition on cancel

If a buyer's fill TX and your cancel TX are both in the mempool at the same time, whichever confirms first wins. If the buyer wins, the trade completes and you receive BTC. If your cancel wins, the asset is refunded and the buyer's TX fails (no BTC sent).

Expiry

Orders expire automatically when the Bitcoin block height reaches creation_block + expiration. The protocol refunds your asset in the same block — no action needed on your part.

Part 03: Filling an Order →
Part 03 — Buyers

Filling an Order

Buying is a single action. Find a listing, review the terms, and sign the fill transaction — the asset arrives in your wallet the moment it confirms, with your BTC going to the seller atomically.

How to Fill an Order

1
Browse the marketplace

Go to Marketplace → Browse and filter by asset, price, or availability. Each listing shows the asset, quantity, total BTC price, and blocks remaining.

2
Select a listing and click Buy

The app fetches the seller's partial PSBT and seller_auth from the relay, and calls POST /v2/compose/atomicfill to build the complete transaction.

3
Review the transaction

Confirm the asset, quantity, and BTC price. The composed PSBT shows exactly what you'll send (BTC) and receive (the asset).

4
Sign and broadcast

Sign the fill transaction with your wallet (SIGHASH_ALL on your inputs). Broadcast to Bitcoin. Once confirmed, the asset is credited to your address.

✅
Zero counterparty risk

You cannot be cheated. The seller's signature locks output[0] — if you underpay or redirect the BTC, the transaction is invalid and won't confirm. Either the trade happens exactly as specified or nothing happens.

Part 05: Security Model →
Part 05 — Under the Hood

Security Model

Atomic Swaps use two independent cryptographic mechanisms that together guarantee neither party can cheat.

SIGHASH_SINGLE | ANYONECANPAY
PSBT signature

The seller's input[0] signature commits to exactly output[0] — the BTC payment to the seller. This makes it impossible to underpay or redirect the BTC.

BIP 340 Schnorr
seller_auth

Signed over the exact trade terms (asset, quantity, price, fill UTXO). The buyer cannot alter any field in the AtomicFill message — the indexer verifies this signature on-chain.

Attack Vectors — and Why They Fail

AttackWhy it fails
Seller moves asset after listingAsset is debited at order creation — it's already in escrow
Buyer modifies price or assetseller_auth verification fails at the indexer
Buyer underpays BTCSIGHASH_SINGLE locks output[0] → TX invalid on-chain
Buyer redirects payment to themselvesSIGHASH_SINGLE locks output[0] recipient → TX invalid
Replay seller_auth on a different orderCommitment includes the fill UTXO txid:vout — unique per order
Two buyers fill the same orderFirst to confirm wins. Second TX double-spends the fill UTXO → invalid
Miner front-runs the fillMust forge seller_auth (impossible) or modify signed inputs (invalid)
Buyer fills expired orderIndexer checks expire_index > block_index → rejected

Failure Modes — Who Loses?

🛡️
Nobody ever loses funds

In every failure scenario — low fee, cancel race, double fill, relay outage — either the trade completes correctly or it doesn't happen. There is no partial state where funds are lost.

ScenarioOutcome
Buyer's fill TX doesn't confirm (fee too low)Nothing happens. Buyer retries or gives up. Nobody loses funds.
Seller cancels while buyer is composingBuyer's TX fails (fill UTXO already spent). BTC never sent.
Two buyers race to fillWinner gets asset. Loser's TX fails cleanly. Nobody loses BTC.
Relay goes offlineOrders still visible on-chain via API. PSBTs may need re-sharing.
Part 06: Order Lifecycle →
Part 06 — Under the Hood

Order Lifecycle

Every atomic order moves through a clear state machine. The fill UTXO is the on-chain anchor — its fate determines the order's outcome.

The Fill UTXO Explained

The fill UTXO is a 546-satoshi dust output created by the seller's AtomicSell TX. It acts as a binding anchor — the asset's fate is tied to this single output:

Fill UTXO spent by AtomicFill TX → asset credited to buyer
Fill UTXO spent by any other TX → implicit cancel → asset refunded to seller
Fill UTXO unspent past expire_index → expiry → asset refunded to seller
Part 07: Transaction Structure →
Part 07 — Under the Hood

Transaction Structure

Two Bitcoin transactions make up a complete atomic swap. Understanding their structure helps developers and power users verify correctness.

Transaction 1 — AtomicSell (Seller)

INPUTS:
  [0] Seller's funding UTXO  ← signed SIGHASH_ALL, standard

OUTPUTS:
  [0] ACME data carrier      ← AtomicSell envelope (P2WSH/OLGA)
  [1] Fill UTXO: 546 sats    ← escrow anchor (fill_vout = 1)
  [2] Change to seller

The indexer processes this by debiting the asset from the seller and recording the order with status = "open".

Transaction 2 — AtomicFill (Buyer)

INPUTS:
  [0] Fill UTXO              ← seller pre-signed (SIGHASH_SINGLE|ANYONECANPAY)
      ↳ commits to output[0] only
  [1..N] Buyer's BTC UTXOs  ← buyer signs (SIGHASH_ALL)

OUTPUTS:
  [0] BTC to seller          ← btc_price sats · locked by seller's sig
  [1] Dust to buyer          ← 546 sats · destination_idx = 1
  [2] ACME data carrier      ← AtomicFill envelope (P2WSH/OLGA)
  [3] Buyer's BTC change
🔑
Why SIGHASH_SINGLE|ANYONECANPAY?

SIGHASH_SINGLE means the seller's signature only covers input[0] and output[0] — so the buyer can freely add more inputs and outputs without invalidating the seller's sig. ANYONECANPAY means anyone can be input[0]'s signer, enabling any buyer to complete the transaction.

The seller_auth Commitment

Before signing the fill PSBT, the seller also signs a commitment hash that ties together all trade terms:

commitment = SHA256(
  "ACME_ATOMIC_SWAP\0"  ← 16-byte domain separator
  give_asset            ← UTF-8 asset name
  "\0"                  ← separator
  give_quantity         ← 8 bytes big-endian u64
  btc_price             ← 8 bytes big-endian u64
  fill_utxo_txid        ← 32 bytes (txid of AtomicSell TX)
  fill_utxo_vout        ← 4 bytes big-endian u32
)

seller_auth = schnorr_sign(seller_private_key, commitment)

Including the fill_utxo_txid:vout makes the auth unique per order — it cannot be replayed across different listings.

Part 08: API Reference →
Part 08 — Under the Hood

API Reference

Four key endpoints power the atomic swap workflow — two for composing transactions and two for the marketplace.

Compose: Create a Listing

POST /v2/compose/atomicsell

{
  "source":        "tb1q_seller...",
  "give_asset":    "ZENBTC",
  "give_quantity": 1,
  "btc_price":     100000000,    // 1 BTC in satoshis
  "expiration":    1000,          // blocks (~7 days)
  "utxos":        [...],
  "fee_rate":      5.0
}

Returns a PSBT to sign, the fill UTXO coordinates, and the commitment_hash to sign as your seller_auth.

Compose: Fill an Order

POST /v2/compose/atomicfill

{
  "order_tx_hash": "abc123...",
  "buyer_address": "tb1q_buyer...",
  "utxos":        [...],
  "fee_rate":      5.0
}

Returns the complete PSBT merging the seller's pre-signed input with the buyer's BTC inputs. Sign with SIGHASH_ALL and broadcast.

Marketplace: Browse Listings

GET /v2/marketplace/listings

Query paramDescription
assetFilter by asset name
min_price / max_priceFilter by satoshi price range
sortprice_asc (default), price_desc, newest
limit / offsetPagination

Marketplace: Submit a Listing

POST /v2/marketplace/listings — called after broadcasting your AtomicSell TX.

{
  "order_tx_hash": "abc123...",
  "partial_psbt":  "cHNidP8B...",  // fill template, base64
  "seller_auth":   "aabbccdd..."   // 64-byte Schnorr sig, hex
}

Query: Order Status

GET /v2/atomic/orders/:tx_hash — returns full order details including fill information when filled.

Glossary

Key terms used throughout this guide.

AtomicSell
Wire message (MSG_ID=15) broadcast by the seller to create an on-chain order. Escrows the asset and creates the fill UTXO.
AtomicFill
Wire message (MSG_ID=16) broadcast by the buyer inside the fill transaction. References the order and carries the seller_auth.
Fill UTXO
A 546-satoshi dust output created by the AtomicSell TX that acts as the escrow anchor. Spending it as an AtomicFill transfers the asset; spending it any other way cancels the order.
seller_auth
A 64-byte BIP 340 Schnorr signature over a commitment hash that includes the asset, quantity, price, and fill UTXO coordinates. Prevents buyers from modifying trade terms.
PSBT
Partially Signed Bitcoin Transaction. The seller creates a partial PSBT signing input[0] (fill UTXO); the buyer completes it by adding BTC inputs and outputs.
SIGHASH_SINGLE
| ANYONECANPAY
Bitcoin sighash type (0x83) used by the seller. Commits only to input[0] and output[0], allowing the buyer to add more inputs and outputs without invalidating the seller's signature.
Commitment hash
The SHA256 digest of the trade terms signed by the seller to produce seller_auth. Uniquely identifies the trade — it includes the fill UTXO txid:vout, preventing auth replay across orders.
expire_index
The Bitcoin block height at which an open order expires. Computed as creation_block + expiration. The indexer automatically refunds the escrowed asset when this block is processed.
Implicit cancel
When the seller spends the fill UTXO in any transaction other than an AtomicFill. The indexer detects this and refunds the asset — no explicit cancel message needed.
Escrow
The state where the seller's asset is debited from their balance but not yet credited to a buyer. The asset is held by the protocol and guaranteed to be returned (via fill, cancel, or expiry).
