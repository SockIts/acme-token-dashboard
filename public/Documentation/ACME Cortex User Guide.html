Creator's Guide

ACME Cortex

Turn your assets into a connected universe. Cortex is ACME's on-chain metadata and relationship system — the connective tissue between your art, your identity, and your collections.

Concepts

The Building Blocks

Every asset on ACME is a standalone piece of data. Cortex lets you connect those pieces — permanently and verifiably on-chain, embedded directly in your Bitcoin transactions.

→ outgoing link
Axon

A relationship you declare from your asset to another. "This artwork is by this artist."

← incoming link
Dendrite

An incoming reference from someone else's asset to yours. "12 artworks all point to my profile."

# label
Tag

A label on your asset for categorization and discovery. Like #pixelart or #limited-edition.

⇌ mutual
Synapse

A confirmed two-way relationship — a verified handshake where both sides independently agree.

💡
Why on-chain?

Cortex connections aren't database labels. They're embedded in Bitcoin transactions — permanent, verifiable, and controlled by no one but you.

Part 01: Artist Profiles →
Part 01

Artist Profiles

A profile is a special asset that represents you — the creator. Other assets reference your profile to establish authorship and build your on-chain portfolio.

Creating a Profile

Go to Create → Profile and complete these steps.

1
Choose your Asset Name

Your permanent on-chain handle — 3–16 uppercase characters. Examples: ALICE, PIXELKING, MORT. This cannot be changed later.

2
Write your Bio and add Social Links

Up to 500 characters. Add Twitter, Discord, website, or any other URL.

3
Upload your Profile Image

Your avatar or logo. Choose a storage tier based on file size and permanence needs.

4
Set fee and sign

Once confirmed on-chain, your profile is live and every artwork you link builds your portfolio automatically.

Storage Tiers

OLGA
≤ 63 KB

ACME's native UTXO encoding. Art data spread across multiple transaction outputs.

OP_RETURN
≤ 100 KB

Stored in OP_RETURN outputs. No dust outputs required.

Witness
≤ 399 KB

Taproot witness script. Largest on-chain capacity available.

Arweave
No limit

Permanent decentralized storage. Best for high-res art and animation.

Why Profiles Matter

🎨
Portfolio Discovery

Anyone browsing your profile sees all your linked works in one place.

✅
Automatic Verification

Issue both your profile and artwork from the same address — ACME auto-connects them with a green verified badge. No extra steps.

🌐
Cross-Chain Identity

Bind additional blockchain addresses to prove you're the same person across chains.

Part 02: Collections →
Part 02

Collections

A collection groups related assets together — a series, a themed drop, a curated gallery. Collections are full on-chain assets with their own metadata and verification.

Creating a Collection

1
Go to Create → Collection

Fill in Asset Name (e.g. SUMMERSERIES), Title, Description, Cover Image, and optionally link your artist profile as Curator.

2
Sign and broadcast

Your collection is now a live, permanent asset on-chain.

Adding Items

🪙
At mint time (easiest)

When creating a new artwork, add a collection relationship. It's embedded permanently in the reveal transaction.

🔗
After creation

Go to Manage Asset on any artwork you own and add a collection relationship via a Cortex Update transaction.

How Membership Works

When your artwork declares collection: MYSERIES, two things happen automatically:

Your Artwork ── [collection] ──→ MYSERIES ← axon (outgoing)
MYSERIES ────── receives ────→ Your Artwork ← dendrite (incoming)

⚠️
Cross-chain links show yellow or gray

The foreign chain can't create a matching axon back to ACME. Use Cross-Chain Attestation (below) to upgrade the badge to blue.

Cross-Chain Attestation

An attestation is a formal on-chain claim about a foreign asset, optionally backed by cryptographic proof.

Claim Types

ClaimMeaning
OwnershipYou own this foreign asset — e.g., you hold a Bored Ape and want to link it to your ACME profile
AuthorshipYou created this foreign asset — e.g., you minted an Ordinal inscription and want to connect it to your ACME artist profile
SameThis foreign asset IS the same work as this ACME asset — the same artwork published on two chains

Proof Levels

gray — Self-Attested
No proof required

Declare the claim with no cryptographic proof. Results in crosschain_claimed status — easiest but least trusted.

blue — Cryptographic
Signed message

Sign a message from the foreign address. Results in crosschain_attested — a much stronger trust signal.

Multi-Chain Identity

Your profile's primary address is auto-linked. If you're active on multiple chains, bind additional addresses to prove they all belong to you — building a unified on-chain identity.

Supported Chains

ChainAddress Format
Bitcoinbc1q... · bc1p... · 1... · 3...
Ethereum0x... (40 hex chars)
Tezostz1... · tz2... · tz3...
SolanaBase58 address
Base & Polygon0x... (same as Ethereum)

How to Bind an Address

1
Sign a message from your foreign wallet

Format: ACME:BIND:MYPROFILE:<timestamp>

2
Submit a ProfileBind transaction

Include your profile name, target chain, foreign address, and the signed message + signature.

3
Done

Result: address_verified. You can also self-attest — the address binds but shows self_claimed.

Why Bind Addresses?

🗂️
Unified portfolio

Collectors visiting your ACME profile see your work across all chains.

🔐
Stronger verification

When your bound address matches an NFT you reference, ACME verifies the link automatically.

🔍
Cross-chain discovery

Other users searching your Ethereum or Solana address find your ACME profile.

Part 07: Verification & Trust →
Part 07

Verification & Trust

When you connect two assets, ACME evaluates the trust level and shows a colored badge — giving collectors an instant read on how well-established each relationship is.

green — Issuer Verified
Same-Address Proof

Both assets issued from the same Bitcoin address. ACME auto-verifies — no extra steps needed.

green — Synapse Verified
Mutual Agreement

Both sides independently created matching relationships toward each other.

yellow — Pending
One-Sided Claim

Only one asset has declared the relationship so far. Normal until the other side confirms.

blue / gray — Cross-Chain
Foreign Reference

Blue = cryptographic proof. Gray = self-attested only. The foreign chain can't auto-confirm.

The Easiest Path to a Green Badge

1
Create your profile from address X
2
Create your artwork from the same address X
3
Add an artist relationship pointing to your profile
4
Done — auto-verified ✓

ACME sees the same issuer on both sides. Same logic applies to collections.

Synapses — Two-Way Confirmation

A synapse forms when both sides of a relationship independently agree — most relevant when different creators collaborate from different addresses.

Your Artwork ── [artist] ──→ Alice's Profile pending
Alice's Profile ── [work] ──→ Your Artwork synapsed

Curated Collections

Collections support a submit → review → approve workflow, entirely on-chain. The synapse system acts as the approval mechanism — no off-chain coordination required.

Multi-Contributor Submission Flow

StepWhoActionResult
1ArtistAdds collection → BTCART2026 to their artworkpending
2CuratorSees the incoming dendrite on the collection's Manage page—
3CuratorCreates item → ARTWORK from the collectionsynapsed
ℹ️
Rejecting a submission

There's no explicit reject. If the curator doesn't create the reverse item axon, the relationship stays yellow. The absence of the green badge is the signal.

Collaborations

Cortex has purpose-built relationship types for multi-artist work — guest credits, commissions, remixes, and endorsements, all verified on-chain.

Attribution Patterns

ScenarioRelationships Used
Solo attributionartist → your profile (auto-verified, same issuer)
Guest artistartist → guest profile; guest confirms with work → piece
Multiple contributorsartist + collaborator → each contributor's profile
Commission recordcommissioner → patron; patron confirms with commissioned
Derivative / remixderivative → original; original may confirm with spawned
🔒
Ownership-Validated Relationships

artist, collaborator, collection, and series require you to own the target asset — preventing false credits. If you don't, the target's owner creates the inverse axon to confirm from their side.

Real-World Example: Collaborative Drop

// Alice: same address as collection — auto-verified
ALICE.PIECE ──[artist]──→ ALICE           ● green ✓ (issuer)
ALICE.PIECE ──[collection]──→ ARTDROP2026 ● green ✓ (issuer)

// Bob: different address — needs curator approval
BOB.PIECE ──[artist]──→ BOB               ● green ✓ (issuer)
BOB.PIECE ──[collection]──→ ARTDROP2026   ● yellow (pending)

// Alice approves by creating the reverse axon
ARTDROP2026 ──[item]──→ BOB.PIECE         → synapse formed ✓ green

Reference

Glossary

Key terms used throughout this guide.

Axon
An outgoing relationship you declare from your asset to another target asset.
Dendrite
An incoming relationship — someone else's asset points to yours. Visible on your asset's detail page.
Synapse
A confirmed two-way relationship where both sides independently created matching axons toward each other.
Inverse Pair
Matching forward/reverse relationship types — e.g., collection↔item — that form a synapse when both exist.
Cortex Update
A transaction that modifies tags or relationships after minting. Up to 50 operations per transaction.
Issuer
The Bitcoin address that created (issued) an asset. Controls the Manage page and relationship editing rights.
Issuer Verified
greenBoth assets were issued from the same Bitcoin address.
Synapse Verified
greenBoth sides created matching axons toward each other — a mutual on-chain handshake.
Pending
yellowOnly one side has declared the relationship. Normal until the other side confirms.
Tag
An on-chain label for categorization and discovery. Up to 50 per asset.
Attribute
A structured trait (key-value pair) — searchable and filterable by collectors. Up to 50 per asset.
Cross-Chain Ref
A relationship pointing to an asset on another blockchain using a URI prefix like /o/ or /eth/.
Profile
A special asset type representing a creator identity. Other assets reference it to claim authorship.
Collection
A special asset type representing a curated group of works, with its own cover image and metadata.
Curator
The creator/owner of a collection who approves submissions by creating item axons, forming synapses.
Ownership Validated
Four types (artist, collaborator, collection, series) require you to own the target asset to prevent false credits.
Endorsement
A social proof relationship from an established artist to another's work, visible to collectors as a trust signal.
